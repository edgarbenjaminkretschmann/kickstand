### Honda VFR 750

I bought this motorbike after intensive research around all the criteria had in mind of what I wanted. I had an exact picture of all my requirements: I wanted a reliable, powerful, ergo- and economic, visually appealing bike, that was in the lower price range. I didn’t care about the age, and after some research I decided for a 95 Honda VFR 750 which I bought for 1300 Euro at that time. Although one gets very used to the crazy amount of power such a motorcycle produces and wants even more, I haven’t once looked back and regretted (past tense regret X) my decision. I started to truly understand how handle my first big bike after about a year, and now I am very much in love with the sound and the sheer joy I was having with it. I only drive it in Europe in the warmer months and use it for commuting as well as some trips. My most exciting trip so far was going through the alps in July 2018. Driving through Bavaria, Austria, Switzerland and France really was a lot of fun. Despite of being alone, I had a great time digging into the curves of lonely passes on 2500 meters altitude. The best roads of all I think, were in Switzerland, also a stunning nature and architecture in the cities it something, that makes Switzerland a very appealing place to live.

Anyways, the bike is an all time favorite for many people but for me it’s also a great bike because it’s cheap in the market, cheap to maintain and cheap to ride (gas per mileage for power output), has a really strong powerband because of it’s V4 outset, sexy sound, balance and agility.

### Rusi KY ZX 200

I bought this bike in October 2019 under pressure of finding something. You see, I’m a great fan of buying about anything used, may it be music instruments, home appliances, consumer electronics, vehicles, about anything – even clothes (more about that later). I find it always a very cheap and in my own way very environmental-friendly to use already produced materials. I also firmly believe that I saved a lot of money over the years with this attitude.

Alright coming back to my RUSI bike. Rusi motorcycles are something like a filippino thing, since the company is founded by a chinese-filippino businessman, and rose to international size. Also, many of the motorcycles sold are assembled in the Philippines and sold to rediculously low prices. Another thing which makes them so popular is their durability and the fact that everybody can afford them and cheap spare parts. Just to have an idea: when I had to have my spark plug changed, it cost my 55 pesos – or about a dollar –, also, if you buy a bike from RUSI, they will have it repaired at any of their workshops for free. A life time of free service so to say included (caveat: you still very much should tip the mechanic, spare parts are on you, and since it’s many times very much in demand, since it’s free there will be a great line.

I bought this particular bike because I wanted to try a dirt-kind-of bike and a higher number of cc, meaning power. Since I was exclusively opting for the secondary market and there are very few merchants who offer used bikes, and although I made a steal buying my Yamaha SZ 150 two years before in the same city, I used Facebook Market to find a bike. Facebook Market by the way is probably the strongest platform to find a used object, since there is no platform like amazon.com or ebay.com. The downside of Fb Market is, that it’s relatively hard to get a grip of the chaotic interface, meaning you first have to invest some time to get a hang of how it works, and there are very few options to filter out the things that are not in your focus. Just like so many other things, it makes looking for things more time-intensive.

The carburator which I bougth at this day, caused major issues alont the ride because of what I believe a maladjustment to the cylinder, it just didn’t fit with this kind of bike. Leaving the city behind us on our first test ride, we already had problems of getting it started and even while riding the bike would just stop going. I bought myself a kit of sandpaper, plies and a screwdriver to be able to independently clean the spark plug. But in the long run, after 1000 km driving and a huge consumption in gasoline, I decided to go for a more economic and reliale 150cc carburator, which another Rusi store replaced the old one on Siquijore.

Looking back at this, the lesson I have learned from this is: -get smart about essential things and -if you have a bad feeling about something, gather more information so you won’t make a mistake and most importantly -don’t buy things under pressure, there will always be another option out there, you just have to find it.

So, I found this bike in some village outside of Iloilo, once we got there you get a real idea of how the majority of filippino people live. But I don’t want to digress on that now. At firswt glance, it looked rusty and unused, despite it’s young age. But nevertheless I thought I came all this way, so I should give it a try. Huffing and puffing it was hard to steer this rather uneasy and heavy bike out of the crowded driveway- the gearing was new to me- but also, something seemed wrong with the carburator. Well, I decided that moment that I wouldn’t be buying this bike, and we returned to the city. The days after, I was constantly looking for a budget bike in the same range. There were some japanese bikes around, but it all didn’t seem in my budget, and so after another week of looking around I decided to give it a go and bought the bike.

I had about two weeks left in Iloilo, and almost daily, I went to the local store to have my new bike repaired, and as we progressed to getting this motorcycle fit for the road, new issues emerged, the biggest being a broken carburator. I asked the manager at the local storem how we could solve this issue, and the only viable solution was ordering a brand new carburator that was obviously a hassle since for a niche bike like this one there was no, replacement. I first said yes, but being under pressure of leaving rather soon, I changed my mind about it and asked them if there was another solution, and there was: an aftermarket 200cc carburator. The mechanic of the Rusi store and I went to a Chinoy (meaning a person of a Pinoy-Chinese descendance) who offered me a ‘certain’ carburator. I had no second guesses but I could feel there was some kind of apprehension with the mechanic. The mainly spoke in hiligaynon – the local language –, so I couldn’t understand a word. Well, as it turned out later, I made my biggest mistake right there and then, but without the expertise, and all circumstances given, there wasn’t much of a choice.

### Yamaha SZ 150

This motorcycle was a fairly good looking and reliable bike. As many bikes in the lower cc range in Asia and clutch driven, it has this big bike look with a lot of plastic and an overdimensioned exhaust. I bought it second hand from a dealer (MOTORTRADE LINK HERE X) in Iloilo: It looked like someone chashed it before and after I bought it, I was told that the previous owner’s wife tried to ruin the bike over some jealousy, but I strongly doubt it since she must have been as strong as hulk to crash about all the plastic on the bike. I had the bike built up and back together in the same shop, and except for the plastics, everything was going smoothly after just a bit of time of repair.

As I found out over the coming 18 months, the japanese built quality is something you will appreciate over the long run, this motorcycle was utterly reliable: It didn’t break down once and I rode across Panay Island, Mindoro, and in and around Manila. and Quezon province.

### Chinese Honda Win Sufat

I am writing this text roughly 5 years after the trip, so I try my best to recollect in the right way, but of what I can tell now is, as time progresses, the distance to the subject gives me a distinctive perspective on things.

I rented this bike In Saigon for my journey through Vietnam with the idea in mind of returning it with 22 days.

In comparison to all the bikes I had I regard this bike as a fairly reliable bike, whih broke down only once a day 😛. The good thing in Vietnam, like in many other Asian countries is, that they have a mechanic right around every corner who fix your bike for less than 10 USD.

So whenever something happened, we just made sure that we would find a way to get the engine started or roll down (if we were in the mountains) into the valley to the next tradesman.
